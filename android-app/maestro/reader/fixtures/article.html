Android 17 brings resizable apps everywhere

Google's next release removes orientation locks on large screens, so apps that assumed a portrait phone now have to adapt to any window the person chooses, from a narrow split screen to a wide desktop window.

For most Compose apps the change is small: window size classes and adaptive panes do the work, and the layout that served a phone simply gains room. Apps that measured the screen once at start-up and never again are the ones that break.

What changes for readers

Reading apps gain the most. A column of text that stays near sixty-five characters per line is easier to follow than a line that runs across a whole tablet, so a reader keeps its column narrow and centres it in the window.

Every app is a large-screen app now, the team said at the developer summit, and the guidance for text is the same as it was for print.

Links inside an article still open in the browser: adaptive layouts in Compose is the starting point the documentation gives.

Three things to check

  • Text stays readable at every font scale the person sets.
  • Controls keep a touch target of at least forty-eight density-independent pixels.
  • Content clears the system bars and the display cutout in every orientation.

The release also brings predictive back to every screen, so a partial swipe shows where the person will land before they lift the finger. Screens that handle back themselves need to say so ahead of time.

  1. Turn on edge-to-edge drawing.
  2. Pad content with the safe drawing insets.
  3. Test the app on a foldable in both postures.
