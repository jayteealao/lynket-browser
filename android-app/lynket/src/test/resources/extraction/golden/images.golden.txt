reader.block.1.text: The surviving evidence for ancient lighthouses is uneven. Simple coastal fires, wooden beacons, and other early navigation markers were less likely to leave durable archaeological remains, while monumental stone towers could survive in ruins or be documented through coins, inscriptions, and written accounts. For this reason, famous structures such as the Pharos of Alexandria are better understood as major early examples of monumental lighthouse construction rather than proof of the first use of navigational lights.
